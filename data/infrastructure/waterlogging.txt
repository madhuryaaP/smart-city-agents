WATERLOGGING

Waterlogging occurs when water accumulates in an area and does not drain away effectively within the expected time.

Waterlogging commonly occurs on roads, streets, low-lying areas, public spaces, and other locations after rainfall.

Drainage conditions, rainfall intensity, surface characteristics, and the surrounding infrastructure can all influence the occurrence of waterlogging.

COMMON CAUSES OF WATERLOGGING

Common causes of waterlogging include:

- Blocked drains
- Poor drainage
- Heavy rainfall
- Insufficient drainage capacity
- Damaged drainage structures
- Low-lying areas
- Obstructed water-flow paths
- Accumulation of waste or debris in drains

Blocked drains can prevent rainwater from flowing through the intended drainage system.

Heavy rainfall can produce large amounts of surface runoff within a short period. If the drainage system cannot carry the water effectively, water may accumulate.

Low-lying areas can be more susceptible to waterlogging because water naturally moves toward lower elevations.

WATERLOGGING AND DRAINAGE

Waterlogging is closely connected with urban drainage.

Drainage systems are designed to collect and carry rainwater away from roads and other developed areas.

If a drain is blocked, damaged, or unable to handle the amount of water entering it, water may remain on the surface and cause waterlogging.

Garbage and other debris can also obstruct drains and reduce the movement of water.

EFFECTS OF WATERLOGGING

Waterlogging can affect roads, traffic movement, pedestrian areas, buildings, and other urban infrastructure.

Water accumulated on roads can make vehicle movement more difficult and may reduce the usable road space.

Prolonged water accumulation can also contribute to deterioration of road surfaces and surrounding infrastructure.

The effect of waterlogging depends on factors such as the amount of water, duration, location, drainage condition, and surrounding infrastructure.

WATERLOGGING AFTER RAINFALL

Waterlogging can become more noticeable during or after heavy rainfall.

A location that normally drains water effectively may experience temporary accumulation during intense rainfall.

If the same location repeatedly becomes waterlogged after rainfall, the drainage system and surrounding infrastructure may require further inspection.

Waterlogging does not always indicate a single specific problem. It may result from rainfall intensity, drainage limitations, blockages, surface conditions, or a combination of factors.

WATERLOGGING PREVENTION AND MAINTENANCE

Keeping drainage systems clear and properly maintained can help reduce water accumulation.

Maintenance may include removing waste and debris, clearing blocked drains, repairing damaged drainage structures, and maintaining intended water-flow paths.

Areas that repeatedly experience waterlogging may require investigation to identify the underlying drainage or infrastructure problem.

REPORTING WATERLOGGING

When reporting waterlogging, provide:

- Location
- Nearby landmark
- Description of the affected area
- Photograph when possible and safe
- Information about the approximate extent of water accumulation
- Information about whether traffic or pedestrian movement is affected
- Information about whether the problem occurs during or after rainfall

Accurate location information helps the responsible authority identify the affected area.

A report can mention whether the water is present on a road, footpath, public space, residential area, or another location.

GENERAL WATERLOGGING KNOWLEDGE

Waterlogging and flooding are related but are not necessarily the same condition.

Waterlogging generally refers to water remaining accumulated in a particular area because it cannot drain away effectively.

The exact cause of waterlogging cannot always be determined from standing water alone. Inspection of drainage systems, surface conditions, and surrounding infrastructure may be required.

Waterlogging can sometimes be temporary and disappear after rainfall stops and the accumulated water drains away.
