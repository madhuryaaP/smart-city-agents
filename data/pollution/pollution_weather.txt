AIR POLLUTION AND WEATHER

Weather and atmospheric conditions can influence the concentration, movement, and distribution of air pollutants.

The same pollution source can produce different measured air-quality conditions at different times because weather conditions can change how pollutants disperse or accumulate.

WIND AND AIR POLLUTION

Wind can transport pollutants away from their original source.

Strong winds can help disperse some pollutants, while wind direction can move pollution from one area toward another.

Wind can also carry dust and other particles from surrounding areas.

Therefore, pollution measured at one location may sometimes be influenced by sources located elsewhere.

RAIN AND AIR POLLUTION

Rain can remove some particles and pollutants from the atmosphere.

Rainfall can temporarily change measured air-quality conditions by reducing the amount of some airborne particles.

However, the effect of rainfall on air quality can vary depending on the pollutant, intensity of rainfall, duration, and other environmental conditions.

Air quality can change again after rainfall if pollution sources continue to release pollutants.

TEMPERATURE

Temperature can influence atmospheric conditions and the behaviour of pollutants.

Changes in temperature can affect the movement and dispersion of air pollutants.

Temperature can also influence chemical reactions in the atmosphere and therefore affect the formation of some pollutants.

SUNLIGHT

Sunlight can influence chemical reactions in the atmosphere.

Some pollutants, including ground-level ozone, can form through chemical reactions involving other pollutants in the presence of sunlight.

As a result, ozone levels can vary depending on sunlight, atmospheric conditions, and the availability of other pollutants.

ATMOSPHERIC CONDITIONS

Atmospheric conditions influence how easily pollutants are dispersed.

When pollutants are not dispersed effectively, their concentrations can increase in a particular area.

Different atmospheric conditions can therefore contribute to changes in air quality even when emission sources remain similar.

POLLUTION DURING DIFFERENT TIMES OF DAY

Air quality can change throughout the day.

Traffic activity, industrial activity, temperature, sunlight, wind, and other conditions can all influence pollution levels.

Morning and evening conditions may differ because traffic patterns and atmospheric conditions can change.

There is no single time of day when pollution is always highest in every location. Local conditions and pollution sources determine the actual pattern.

SEASONAL CHANGES

Air-quality conditions can also vary between seasons.

Seasonal changes in weather, wind, temperature, rainfall, human activities, and other factors can affect pollution levels.

The dominant pollution sources can also change between seasons.

WEATHER AND AQI

Weather conditions can influence the pollutant measurements used to calculate or report AQI.

As pollutant concentrations change, the reported AQI can also change.

AQI should therefore be considered together with the location and time of measurement.

LOCAL DIFFERENCES

Weather conditions can contribute to differences in air quality between locations.

Two areas in the same city can have different pollution levels because of differences in wind, traffic, local emissions, land use, and surrounding conditions.

GENERAL KNOWLEDGE

Weather does not create all air pollution, but it can strongly influence how pollutants move, disperse, accumulate, or react in the atmosphere.

Current weather conditions should not be used alone to determine the current AQI. Actual air-quality measurements are required for current pollution information.

The Smart City application should use current air-quality data for real-time pollution conditions and can use general knowledge about weather to explain possible changes in pollution.
