AIR POLLUTION AND AQI

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is used to communicate the level of air pollution in a way that is easier for the public to understand.

AQI provides a simplified indication of air-quality conditions based on measured air pollutants and the applicable AQI calculation system.

AQI categories can be used to describe air quality from relatively clean conditions to increasingly polluted conditions.

The exact numerical ranges and category definitions can vary depending on the AQI standard and data source being used.

GOOD AQI

Good air quality indicates that air pollution is generally at a low level according to the applicable AQI system.

Air quality is considered satisfactory and air pollution poses little or no risk under this category.

Normal outdoor activities can generally continue while following any applicable local guidance.

MODERATE AQI

Moderate air quality indicates that pollution levels are higher than the good category but remain within the moderate range of the applicable AQI system.

Air quality may be acceptable, but unusually sensitive people may experience some effects.

People who are sensitive to air pollution should pay attention to current air-quality information and applicable local guidance.

POOR AIR QUALITY

Poor air quality indicates a higher level of air pollution.

People who are sensitive to air pollution may experience health effects and should consider reducing prolonged outdoor exposure.

Current AQI information and applicable local health recommendations should be considered when deciding about prolonged outdoor activities.

VERY POOR AIR QUALITY

Very poor air quality indicates a further increase in pollution levels.

Health effects may become more noticeable, and people should consider limiting prolonged outdoor activities.

People who are sensitive to air pollution should pay particular attention to applicable local health guidance.

SEVERE AIR POLLUTION

Severe air pollution indicates a high level of pollution according to the applicable AQI system.

Air pollution can pose a significant health risk.

People should follow local health guidance and reduce exposure to polluted outdoor air.

Current air-quality information should be monitored when available.

AQI AND POLLUTANTS

AQI is related to measurements of air pollutants such as:

- PM2.5
- PM10
- Nitrogen dioxide
- Sulfur dioxide
- Carbon monoxide
- Ozone

AQI is an index and should not be confused with the concentration of an individual pollutant.

For example, a PM2.5 measurement represents the concentration of particulate matter, while AQI communicates air-quality conditions using an index.

The pollutant contributing most significantly to an AQI value can depend on the location, time, measurements, and AQI calculation method.

AQI CHANGES

AQI can change throughout the day because pollutant concentrations can change.

Traffic, industrial activity, construction, weather, wind, rainfall, sunlight, and other environmental conditions can influence air quality.

AQI can also differ between different locations in the same city.

Therefore, an AQI value from one location and time should not automatically be assumed to represent the current condition of an entire city.

USING AQI INFORMATION

AQI information can help users understand air-quality conditions without requiring them to interpret multiple pollutant measurements individually.

The Smart City application can use AQI information to provide users with understandable pollution-related recommendations.

When presenting AQI information, the application should consider the location, time of measurement, AQI standard, and data source.

STATIC INFORMATION AND REAL-TIME AQI

Static knowledge can explain what AQI categories mean and provide general information about air pollution.

Real-time AQI values should come from a current air-quality data source rather than being generated from the static knowledge base.

The application should distinguish between general explanations of AQI categories and current measured AQI values.

A static knowledge file cannot determine the current AQI of a location unless current measurements are provided by a live data source.

GENERAL AQI KNOWLEDGE

A higher AQI generally indicates greater air pollution within the applicable AQI system.

A lower AQI generally indicates better air-quality conditions.

AQI categories provide a simplified way of communicating pollution conditions, but the exact interpretation depends on the AQI standard being used.

AQI information should be considered together with the measurement location, measurement time, underlying pollutant data, and applicable local guidance.

The Smart City application can combine current AQI measurements with the knowledge base to explain what the reported air-quality condition means in simple language.
