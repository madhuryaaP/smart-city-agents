EFFECTS OF AIR POLLUTION

Air pollution can affect people, animals, plants, ecosystems, buildings, and the wider environment. The effects can vary depending on the type of pollutant, concentration, duration of exposure, and environmental conditions.

EFFECTS ON THE ENVIRONMENT

Air pollution can contribute to environmental problems such as:

- Reduced air quality
- Reduced visibility
- Damage to vegetation
- Effects on ecosystems
- Changes in environmental conditions
- Deposition of pollutants on soil and water

Different pollutants can have different effects on the environment.

EFFECTS ON VISIBILITY

High concentrations of particles and other pollutants in the atmosphere can reduce visibility.

Reduced visibility can make it more difficult to see distant objects and can affect road and transportation conditions.

Dust, smoke, particulate matter, and other atmospheric particles can contribute to reduced visibility.

EFFECTS ON PLANTS

Air pollutants can affect vegetation and plant growth.

The effect can depend on the type of pollutant, concentration, duration of exposure, plant species, and environmental conditions.

Some pollutants can interfere with normal plant processes when present at sufficiently high concentrations.

EFFECTS ON ANIMALS

Animals can also be affected by polluted air and changes in their surrounding environment.

The effects can vary depending on the pollutant, level of exposure, duration, species, and environmental conditions.

Air pollution can also affect animals indirectly by affecting vegetation, water, soil, and ecosystems.

AIR POLLUTION AND WATER

Some air pollutants can eventually be deposited onto land or water surfaces.

Pollutants deposited from the atmosphere can affect environmental systems depending on the type and amount of pollutant.

The relationship between air pollution and water quality can therefore involve atmospheric deposition as well as other environmental processes.

AIR POLLUTION AND SOIL

Pollutants can be deposited onto soil through atmospheric processes.

The effect on soil depends on the pollutant, amount deposited, soil characteristics, and environmental conditions.

AIR POLLUTION AND BUILDINGS

Air pollution can contribute to the deterioration or soiling of buildings and other structures.

Particles and certain pollutants can accumulate on surfaces over time.

The effect can vary depending on the pollutant, building materials, environmental conditions, and duration of exposure.

SHORT-TERM AND LONG-TERM EFFECTS

Air-pollution effects can occur over different time periods.

Short-term effects are associated with exposure over relatively short periods, while long-term effects can result from repeated or prolonged exposure.

The type and severity of effects depend on the pollutant, concentration, duration of exposure, and individual or environmental factors.

URBAN AIR POLLUTION

Urban air pollution can affect roads, public spaces, vegetation, buildings, and other parts of the urban environment.

Pollution levels can vary between different parts of a city because of traffic, industries, construction, weather, and other local factors.

MONITORING POLLUTION EFFECTS

Air-quality monitoring can help identify changes in pollutant concentrations and pollution patterns.

Monitoring information can be combined with environmental observations to better understand how air pollution affects an area.

The Smart City application can provide general information about possible environmental effects while using current monitoring data for actual pollution conditions.

GENERAL KNOWLEDGE

Air pollution does not have one single effect. Different pollutants can affect different parts of the environment in different ways.

The effects of air pollution depend on the pollutant, concentration, duration, location, weather, and other environmental conditions.

A measured increase in a pollutant does not by itself identify every environmental effect. Detailed environmental assessment may require additional information.
