GENERAL AIR POLLUTION GUIDANCE

When air pollution levels are high, people can reduce exposure by following applicable local health guidance.

Air-quality conditions can change throughout the day and can vary between locations. General pollution guidance should therefore be considered together with current air-quality information when available.

GENERAL CONSIDERATIONS

General considerations during periods of higher air pollution include:

- Reduce prolonged outdoor exposure when pollution is high.
- Follow official local health recommendations.
- Pay particular attention to guidance intended for people who are sensitive to air pollution.
- Monitor current air-quality information when available.
- Consider reducing unnecessary time spent in areas with visibly high pollution or heavy emissions.
- Keep track of changes in local air-quality conditions when planning outdoor activities.

The appropriate response to air pollution can depend on the current pollution level, local conditions, official guidance, and individual circumstances.

SENSITIVE GROUPS

Some people may be more sensitive to air pollution than others.

People who are sensitive to air pollution should pay particular attention to applicable local health recommendations when air-quality conditions are poor.

The application should avoid making assumptions about an individual's health condition and should direct users toward appropriate official guidance when specific health advice is required.

OUTDOOR ACTIVITIES

When pollution levels are high, reducing prolonged outdoor exposure can help reduce contact with polluted air.

Outdoor activities can be planned according to current air-quality information when such information is available.

Pollution conditions may vary by location, so current information for the relevant area is more useful than relying only on a general city-wide assumption.

AIR-QUALITY MONITORING

Monitoring current air-quality information can help users understand changing pollution conditions.

AQI and pollutant measurements can provide information about the current or recently measured air-quality condition.

Users should consider the measurement location and time because air-quality conditions can change.

STATIC KNOWLEDGE AND REAL-TIME DATA

General pollution guidance can be stored in the application's static knowledge base and retrieved through the knowledge system.

Real-time air-quality measurements should come from a current data source rather than from the static knowledge base.

The static knowledge base can explain concepts such as air pollution, AQI, pollutants, and general exposure-reduction considerations.

A real-time data source is required to provide current information such as the latest AQI, current PM2.5 level, current PM10 level, or current pollutant measurements.

The application should clearly distinguish between general guidance and real-time measurements.

PRESENTING POLLUTION INFORMATION

The Smart City application should present pollution information clearly and in a way that is understandable to users.

When current measurements are available, the application should identify that the information comes from a current data source.

General guidance should not be presented as if it were a live pollution measurement.

Similarly, a previous measurement should not automatically be described as the current air-quality condition.

USING OFFICIAL INFORMATION

When specific health recommendations are required, users should follow applicable official local health guidance.

The application can provide general informational guidance but should not replace official health recommendations or professional medical advice.

GENERAL POLLUTION GUIDANCE

Air pollution can vary depending on location, time, emissions, weather, and atmospheric conditions.

There is no single action that applies to every pollution situation. Appropriate precautions depend on the current air-quality condition and applicable local guidance.

Reducing unnecessary prolonged exposure during periods of high pollution and staying informed about current air-quality conditions are general considerations.

The Smart City application should use static knowledge for general explanations and current data sources for real-time pollution information.
